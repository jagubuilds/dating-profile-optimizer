# Per-app notes

Last reviewed: October 2026. Features change often - confirm anything important against the app's help centre or a screen recording of the current screen. Claims marked "reported" come from third-party guides, not the app itself.

## Hinge
- 6 photos/videos, 3 text prompts (~150 characters each), optional voice prompt.
- Free users get a small daily like budget (around 8). Use all of them, every day - inactive accounts reportedly get shown less.
- **Comment on every like.** Hinge says likes with a comment do better; a bare like is easy to ignore.
- Free weekly Rose: use it every week on a Standout, with a specific comment.
- Show "dating intention" honestly; "Long-term, open to short" is the widest honest setting for someone open to both.
- Hide clutter fields (star sign etc.) unless they mean something to the user.
- Widening age range or distance a notch often does more than any rewrite.
- Expect weeks 1-2 to be slow after a big change; judge at week 4.
- In markets where men heavily outnumber women, men receive few inbound likes - that's normal; their matches come mostly from likes they send.

## Bumble
- As of Bumble's help page (updated August 2026): **both people get one opening message**; the match expires if neither sends one within 72 hours. "Opening Moves" are no longer part of the app (older guides still describe them).
- Prompt answers: 160 characters. Prompts can't carry an attached photo, so a prompt that references a photo needs that photo in the 6 slots.
- Some old prompts disappear from the picker but stay on profiles - replace them.
- Complete every field (bio, location, hometown, smoking, family plans, qualities, interests) - Bumble says profile details, activity and verification feed its matching.
- Get photo and ID verification. ID verification requires the user's own government ID - they must do it themselves.
- Connect Spotify and show artists - Bumble highlights shared music, which is a free opener.
- Message first, same day, with something specific from their profile. Reportedly, swiping right on everyone hurts ranking.

## The League
- Referral/application based; aimed at career-driven professionals; small daily batch of profiles instead of endless swiping.
- Reported: tracks flaking - let matches expire or ignore them and visibility drops. Reply to every match within 24 hours, don't unmatch without reason.
- Reported photo rules: no sunglasses, no group shots, at least one full-body photo. A short optional video reportedly gets priority.
- Work and education are prominent and filterable; a named employer/founder title is a strong signal here. Keep LinkedIn current - it's pulled in.
- Prompt answers may be very short (40 characters seen) - see the tight-space tactics in `copy.md`.
- Pools in newer cities are small: be specific, not polarising.

## Tinder
- Photos carry almost everything; lead photo rules apply doubly.
- Short bio; one specific hook beats a list.
- Verification badge helps trust.

## Any app
- A complete profile, a verified badge and daily activity are the common denominators.
- Ask for the user's real settings (age range, distance, filters) - over-narrow filters are a silent match-killer.
- Never recommend bots, auto-swipers or AI-generated photos.
