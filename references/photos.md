# Photo rubric

## The first photo decides most swipes
It needs: clear face, eyes visible (no sunglasses), good natural light, solo, a relaxed or genuine expression. If no existing photo meets this, that is the single highest-value fix - prescribe a quick shoot (below) rather than settling.

## A good set of 6
1. Clear face, eyes visible - the opener
2. Full body (clothed) - builds trust
3. Lifestyle / context - where their life happens (café, city, trip)
4. Warmth - a pet, family or friends where they are obviously identifiable
5. Interest or activity - something people can comment on
6. Personality / wildcard - humour, a running joke, a conversation starter

If one strong photo covers two jobs, fine. Six good photos beat six slots filled with weak ones.

## Red flags (drop or move to the back)
- Sunglasses on the lead photo (some curated apps reportedly penalise sunglasses anywhere)
- Mirror and gym selfies, bathroom/sauna shots
- Shirtless shots, especially early in the set (a scenic, activity-framed one late in the set is the user's call)
- Group photos where it isn't obvious who they are; group shots as the lead
- Screenshots of other apps or social posts (visible UI looks like an ad)
- Memes or other people's photos - viewers assume it's someone else
- Photos where the face is tiny or hidden (silhouettes, far-away action shots) - fine late in the set, never first
- Photos with someone who could be read as an ex
- Props or accessories that read younger than intended if the user wants to come across as mature

## Variety checks people miss
- **Same outfit in several photos.** Viewers notice. Aim for no more than 2 photos in the same top.
- **No smile anywhere.** At least one photo should show a real smile.
- **All selfies.** At least some should be taken by someone else.
- **All one setting.** Mix day/night, indoor/outdoor, city/nature.

## Backing up the copy
Photos and prompts should support each other: a prompt about snow or beaches is stronger next to a snowboarding or beach photo; a running joke in a prompt can point at a specific photo. Check the app allows the pairing - some apps let you attach a photo to a prompt, others don't.

## Cropping
If a good photo has a problem at the edges (other people, logos, clutter), crop to 4:5 portrait with the head in the upper third. Check the crop before recommending it - a crop that's too tight to exclude other people is often worse than a different photo. Selfies may carry mirrored EXIF orientation; verify the crop visually.

## The 10-minute photo fix (when they have no good lead photo)
- Late-afternoon daylight or beside a large window
- Phone portrait mode at 2x, about 2 metres away, chest-up
- A friend makes them laugh instead of "smile for the camera"; take 30-50, keep one
- No friend available: lean the phone at chest height, 10-second timer, burst mode, look just past the lens
- Wear a colour different from the existing photos
