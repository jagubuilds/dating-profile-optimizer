# After the match

Goal: a fun, short chat that turns into a date. Most app chats die from lack of momentum, not from a bad message.

## Openers
- Reference something specific in their profile: a photo, a prompt, a shared interest.
- If they reacted to one of the user's photos or prompts, build on that first.
- One or two lines, ending in something easy to answer.

## Running bits
When they play along with a joke, keep it alive by **pushing it one step further each message and handing it back**:
1. Escalate the premise (e.g. they tease you back → turn it into a mock "case", a "contract" or a "rivalry" with its own little storyline)
2. Let them build on it (they play their part in the storyline)
3. Turn the bit into the date invite (the storyline's "resolution" is meeting up - e.g. a "settlement", a "rematch", a "tasting")
4. Close with a concrete day ("Deal - but we sign it in person. Thursday?")

If they stop building on the bit, drop it and ask a normal question.

## Rules of thumb
- Reply within a few hours - especially if the profile jokes about replying late; the contrast is part of the charm.
- Match their message length and emoji use.
- Suggest meeting after roughly 4-8 good exchanges. Long text-only chats usually stall.
- If they answer one-word twice in a row, make one clear invite; if it's not taken up, let it go gracefully.
- Never pressure, guilt or argue. A "no" or silence is an answer.

## How to help the user mid-chat
Ask them to paste the last few messages. Give 2-3 reply options in their established voice, a recommended pick with the reason, and what to send next depending on how the other person responds.
